What is Green ICT?

Technology solves problems, but it creates them too. Green ICT is about making sure the tools we rely on don't cost us the planet we live on.

What exactly is Green ICT?

Green Information and Communication Technology or Green ICT for short is the environmentally responsible use, management, and disposal of technology. It covers everything from designing eco-friendly products to using devices wisely and disposing of them properly, all to protect both people and the environment.

Why does any of this matter?

Earth's resources are finite. Human activity already drives pollution, climate change, and resource depletion. The ICT sector alone produces roughly 2–3% of global carbon emissions and that number keeps climbing as more of life moves online.If we don't manage how technology is made, used, and discarded,the very tools meant to help us will end up harming us.

Does technology help or hurt the environment?

Both. On the positive side, digital tools reduce paper waste, improve energy management,and let us work more efficiently. Online meetings even cut travel emissions. But manufacturing and running devices consumes energy and raw materials,and discarded electronics pile up as e-waste. Technology isn't the problem using it irresponsibly is.

What are the goals of Green ICT?

Four core goals:
· Reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions from devices and infrastructure;
· Minimize e-waste by extending device lifespans;
· Cut harmful materials and manage finite resources wisely in manufacturing;
· Use digital tools to promote environmental protection and sustainability.

What can students do?

Plenty.Turn off devices and enable sleep modes when not in use. Go digital with notes and assignments to cut paper waste.Take care of laptops and phones repair instead of replacing. And never toss old batteries or gadgets in the trash; bring them to designated e-waste recycling points.

What can schools do?

Schools can power down computers and projectors when idle, reduce paper by distributing assignments and announcements digitally, and repair or recycle old equipment instead of discarding it. Teaching students and staff to use technology responsibly turns the school itself into a Green ICT practice.

Myth vs Fact

These are some things that many believe but the reality turned out to be more complicated. ( We found them on the internet )

Myth

Planting trees cancels out my phone usage.

Fact

A tree absorbs about 22 kg of CO₂ per year. Making a single phone emits 55 to 75 kg. You would need to keep a tree growing for three years just to offset one phone's manufacturing, before counting daily charging.

Myth

Electric cars have zero environmental impact because they don't burn gas.

Fact

EVs produce no exhaust fumes, but manufacturing their batteries is energy-intensive and mining the required lithium, cobalt, and nickel has its own environmental and human cost. They are cleaner overall, but not impact-free.

Myth

Digital is always greener than paper.

Fact

Going digital saves trees, but servers, networks, and devices still consume energy. Streaming an hour of video in HD generates roughly 0.4 kg of CO₂. For short documents, paper can sometimes have a lower footprint than the energy to store and transmit them digitally for years.

Myth

Putting my phone in airplane mode saves a lot of energy.

Fact

Airplane mode does reduce battery drain by stopping wireless transmissions, which means less frequent charging. But the energy saved is small compared to the manufacturing footprint. The bigger win is keeping your phone for an extra year instead of upgrading.

Myth

Recycling my old phone doesn't really do anything.

Fact

One million recycled phones yield roughly 16 tonnes of copper, 350 kg of silver, 34 kg of gold, and 15 kg of palladium. Proper recycling recovers metals that would otherwise need to be mined, which is far more energy-intensive and environmentally damaging.

Myth

The cloud is clean because it is just invisible data in the sky.

Fact

The cloud is physical servers in massive data centres running on electricity, much of it from fossil fuels. A single data centre can use as much electricity as a small city. "The cloud" is a marketing word, not an energy-free one.

Key terms

E-waste
Discarded electronic devices, the world's fastest-growing waste stream.
Carbon footprint
The total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly or indirectly by a person, device, or activity.
Digital footprint
The environmental cost of data, every email, stream, and upload consumes energy somewhere.
Renewable energy
Power from sources that don't run out, sunlight, wind,and water producing little to no emissions.
