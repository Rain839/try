Technology Shouldn't Cost The EARTH.

The computers, networks, and data centres we rely on every day come with a hidden cost to the environment. Green ICT is about facing that cost honestly and changing how we design, use, and retire technology so the digital world treads more lightly on the physical one.

The problems ICT creates

Information and communication technology has transformed how we live and work. But that progress has a footprint most of us never see.

Heavy energy demand

Data centres, networks, and billions of devices run around the clock, drawing electricity that is still largely generated from fossil fuels. ICT now accounts for roughly two percent of global greenhouse gas emissions, and that share keeps climbing.

A growing mountain of e-waste

Each year the world produces over fifty million tonnes of discarded electronics. Much of it ends up in landfill, where toxic components leach into soil and water, and valuable metals are lost rather than recovered.

Manufacturing impact

Building a single laptop can emit hundreds of kilograms of carbon dioxide and consume scarce metals and water. Short upgrade cycles mean most of a device's lifetime footprint is created before it is ever switched on.

Throwaway culture

Devices are increasingly hard to repair and quickly dropped from support, so working hardware is scrapped long before the end of its useful life, deepening both waste and emissions.

The goals of Green ICT

Green ICT sets out to shrink that footprint across the whole life of technology, from factory to recycling bin. Its aims are simple and practical.

01

Reduce energy consumption

Run hardware and software more efficiently, and power it with cleaner electricity wherever possible.

02

Keep devices in service longer

Repair, refurbish, and reuse rather than replace, so the emissions baked into manufacturing pay off over more years.

03

Recover and recycle responsibly

Keep electronics out of landfill and reclaim the metals and materials inside them through certified recycling.

04

Promote sustainability

Write code that needs less power and less hardware, and shift work to times and places where the grid is cleaner.

None of this is about giving up technology. It is about using it with care, so the tools we depend on do not cost the earth we live on.
